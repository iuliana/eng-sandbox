# How to be a Good Manager

**Not everyone will like you and that's okay. Don't fight it, just try to be consistent and fair.**

Be ready for the identity shift - you will no longer be congratulated for getting things done, you will be congratulated for your team getting things done.

A quiet team is not always a stable team. Sometimes silence means people no longer feel able to speak.

Learn the difference between:

* **I don’t know** - I don’t know the answer to that.
* **I can’t say** - I know, but I can’t tell you. And never lie to your team.

The relationship with your peers will change. Once you become a manager, you need to give them time to bond.

When you say anything, even to good friends it has the weight of coming from the company. You have to change/redefine some relationships.

**Give respect, get respect.** Respect your team members, you may know people have areas of improvement, strong points, etc. don’t use those against them. Bolster where they are strong, teach them where they need to improve.

**People are messy, it's not always their fault.**

**FEEDBACK** - give honest, but not mean feedback. Give feedback immediately, when something goes awry **look for the lesson** in it.

**Document everything** -  if you offhandedly tell someone something, you and the company are liable. Someone, somewhere will bite you of you don’t.

!! Don’t bad mouth your leadership to your team. **You lose respect** from the team and someone will share it eventually.

Compliment people, the team, take the responsibility of failures. When a team member does something great. Name-drop them, say “Bob really came though on this”. When your team drops the ball, it’s on you. “I let this slip, slide, etc. I know how to address it, and will do so.”

Some of your employees make more money than you. That is ok.

**Have an open door policy**, let your team come to you, but don’t get down in the muck if they are badmouthing someone. **Listen, document and let them know they’ve been listened to and heard.**

Don't promise what you can't deliver!

Don't be a doormat!

Be okay with people close to you not being your buddy anymore.

**When you yell at someone on your team, or override their opinion the team loses some respect for you, and gain it for the person.** 

Have disagreements before closed doors.

Do NOT bail on one-on-ones. And if you don’t have them scheduled, get them scheduled.

Bad managers think that their job is to make sure their team is working. Good managers realize, it is the managers job to solve any issues that stop them from being their best.

**Your job is no longer to do the work well**, it’s to make sure the work happens. That mental shift takes time, especially when you were just one of the team.

Also, talk to your team more than you think you should. Not just tasks but how they’re doing, what’s unclear, where they’re stuck. You’ll learn faster that way than trying to figure it out alone. And give yourself a bit of a runway. Two weeks is nothing. You’re still calibrating, like you said. It starts feeling more natural after a couple of months once patterns kick in.

**Always get the two sides of a story.**

You must **immediately begin managing up** by creating a list of everyone who now has an opinion on your performance and asking them directly how you can help or where you might be hampering them. 

**Figure out what each team member needs to do their best work.**

My biggest advice is focus on being a kind manager, and not a nice manager. Don't have yourself focus on "how do I get everyone to like me" and instead focus on "how do I get them to respect me". And they will respect you if you approach management with honesty, willingness to be open for being challenges, willingness to show your team members that you will prioritize their career growth and listen to what is important for them, and earning their respect through your actions and not the new title.

**Commend quickly and correct quickly. Set clear expectations.**

Other sidenotes:

* make work visible (who owns what, where things are at) 
* push decisions back to where the context sits instead of absorbing them 
* set a simple check-in rhythm so things don’t stack up silently
* Always **keep the bigger picture in mind** and keep driving towards that goal. Don't let the day-to-day problems and issues drag you down.
    * What is the bigger picture?
        * The wellbeing and growth of people
        * The success of the company
* You need to understand that **your job has to be more important to you than anyone else's**.
* Don't let people get away with stuff they shouldn't just because you feel bad for them for whatever reason. People will always have excuses, they'll try to guilt you, and take advantage of you. You can be lenient and cut people slack when it's appropriate, but not at the cost of violating policy or showing unfair treatment because when(not if) the truth comes out it'll be your head on the chopping block.
* Build or upgrade systems for them and then manage the systems. Clean systems help provide clarity on what’s expected and what delivery should look like.
* Study managers that are good at their job. You need to have a calming effect on other people.
* **Managing well and doing the work well are two completely different skills.**
* Stop asking "did I do the work right?" when the new question is "Did I set my people up to do the work right?"
